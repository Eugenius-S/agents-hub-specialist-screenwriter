# Anti-signs of AI text

Check: read it aloud. If a living person would not say the phrase to a colleague, rewrite it. Do not scrub every sign in a row: too many bans give wooden text, fix the most visible (the "not X but Y" contrast, dashes, triads). [sec] blakestockton.com/p/takeaways-from-wikipedias-signs-of-ai-writing-2

English. Basis: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing (a descriptive list by WikiProject AI Cleanup, not rules), plus humanizer (MIT).
1. Inflated significance: "stands as a testament", "plays a vital role", "underscores", "watershed moment", "leaves a lasting impact".
2. The tone of a travel brochure: "boasts", "vibrant", "rich", "groundbreaking", "nestled", "in the heart of", "breathtaking", "diverse array".
3. The contrast "It's not just X, it's Y" and the trailing negation. The most visible sign.
4. The rule of three: "adjective, adjective, adjective" and three short phrases in a row.
5. Swapping "is" for "serves as", "boasts"; running through synonyms of a word instead of repeating it.
6. False ranges "from X to Y", superficial -ing analyses ("highlighting, showcasing").
7. AI vocabulary: delve, tapestry, landscape, pivotal, testament, foster.
8. Dashes, bold type for decoration, lists with a bold heading, emoji in text.
9. Stock connectors "moreover", "furthermore"; vague references to "experts believe".
10. Chatbot leftovers: "Great question!", "I hope this helps", caveats about the knowledge date.
11. A closing conclusion "the future looks bright", a single aphoristic line at the end.

Russian, when the voice-over is in Russian (the phrases are given here in translation). No single Russian-language primary source was found; this list comes from practice.
1. A dash instead of a comma or a colon, long chains through dashes.
2. Parcelling: chopped phrases of one word each, "Simple. Fast. Convenient."
3. A false antithesis "not X but Y" when nobody claimed X.
4. An objection with no premise: "Many think that... But that is not so."
5. Chewing it over: "Simply put", "in other words", "and that means that".
6. Empty openers: "let us figure it out", "it is important to understand that", "in the modern world", "it is worth noting".
7. "No fluff", "quality", "reliable", "effective", "unique" with no proof.
8. Forced triads: three uniform members so that it sounds weighty.
9. Assistant lines: "of course!", "here is a version", "hope that helped".
10. Bureaucratese and verbal nouns: "a search is carried out" instead of "we search".

For voice-over also: one sentence on screen with no period at the end (an owner edit), no teaser question like "Did you know?" and no greeting. The concrete instead of the evaluative: instead of "convenient search" show the search and name the action.
