# How to come up with ideas

Read before every set of ideas. The owner's edit outranks it.

## A strong idea

A hero or a recognizable format + a situation with tension + a twist + the offer as the resolution.

- **A hero** is a person with a role recognized in a second (a lottery host, a teleshopping seller, a designer who
  looks for an icon on Friday evening). Without a hero what is left is an object metaphor or a screen recording with captions.
- **A situation**: something goes not as the viewer expects, or grows to absurdity. Not "we show functions".
- **A twist**: by the end something changes beyond the logo appearing: the repetition breaks, the roles flip, the stakes
  grow.
- **The offer is the resolution, not a caption.** It answers the situation: "a lottery where you cannot lose" is both the host's joke and
  the wheel's mechanic.

Check: it retells in one phrase with an "and then"; the first frame is visible; you want to forward it to a colleague.

## Devices

Each with an example for a reward-based ad on a catalog page. Take the device, not the example.

1. **A parody of a familiar format.** A TV show, teleshopping ("But wait, there's more!"), an unboxing and "what I bought", a cooking
   show, a sports commentator, a nature documentary, "POV", "a day in the life". The format gives energy and expectation, and the offer
   sits at its climax. Teleshopping itself asks for a bundle: "and that is not all" on every product that is in the
   subscription.
2. **The rule of three with a break.** One, two, and the third time it breaks or takes off. The host spins the drum: "You win!",
   again "You win!", the third time he does not believe it himself.
3. **Literally.** The offer's words are taken literally and played out. "You cannot lose": a host who cannot manage to
   make a loser. "No ads": a video that skips itself.
4. **Straight to camera, to one segment.** A person speaks in the viewer's words about their situation, then the promise. "You already
   pay fifteen bucks a month, right? What if..." Works when the segment is named precisely.
5. **The fourth wall.** A reward-based video knows it is watched for the sake of an icon. The hero should say so, not a caption
   on a screen recording.
6. **Price against a pile.** Ordinary purchases next to what the subscription gives, and the subscription's pile is the biggest. It fits only if
   the pile is shown truthfully: check what is in the plan in the product reference.
7. **Hyperbole of the viewer's moment.** The situation they sit in now taken to absurdity: a queue of ad
   videos before the icon, a colleague waiting for a mockup. The situation is funny, not the person.

A parody hero can be bright. The calm tone from the owner's edits is about the UGC hero who shares a find.

## Order

1. Inputs: who watches at the moment of the show and why they are here; what the offer changes for exactly them, from the product reference
   and prices, not the brief's numbers. A headline discount from the monthly price tells a free user nothing; to a monthly payer it does.
2. Quickly 20-30 raw logline strings by different devices, do not show them.
3. Cross out: the skeleton "screen recording + caption + wheel + logo"; an object metaphor with no person (a fortune
   cookie, a weather forecast, a receipt, a lottery ticket in big); ideas where the offer is simply spoken; ideas that resemble
   each other.
4. Bring the remaining ones to two paragraphs in the owner's format and score them against the checklist below. Show the 3-5 best.

## Checklist before showing

- Is there a hero with a role or a recognizable format?
- Is there a twist, and is it not the logo?
- Is the offer the resolution, and is it correct: price, term, what is in the plan (the product reference), for whom?
- Will the viewer at the moment of the show understand the first 3 s without sound?
- Are the ideas in the set different by device, rather than one idea ten times?
- Is the product shown with real screens in pieces inside the video, rather than a phone in the last frame?
- Is the offer's mechanic (a wheel, a bonus) part of the joke or one line of the finale? The joke works without it: do not glue it on
  as a separate scene: that is the same skeleton "... + wheel + logo".
- No more than two format parodies in a set; an off-screen commentator (documentary, sport) is one device, not two.
- The UGC hero is in frame only in the hook and the finale; a parody host holds the video, but the product screens are inside.

Delivery: each idea is a title and two paragraphs, with no lines "Device:", "Hook:" and no explanations. A question whose
answer is in the product reference or in the brief, do not ask: give a version for each segment at once.

## Breakdowns of strong videos

Take the device, not the plot. Sources are in the links.

- **Geico "Unskippable" (2015).** "You cannot skip this ad: it is already over": the pitch in the first 5 s, then
  a family freezes in frame while the dog eats their dinner. The fourth wall + literally. Pre-roll is skipped after 5 s in 96% of cases,
  this one was watched past 30 s; a Cannes Grand Prix. campaignlive.com/article/.../1385546
- **Geico "Fast Forward" (2016).** An ordinary scene, and then "let us fast forward to the end so you get back to your video sooner":
  the finale is absurd. The ad itself says it is watched for something else: this is the reward-based situation as it is.
- **Duolingo "Dead Duo" (2025).** The owl "died waiting for you to do a lesson". One hyperbole of the user's moment,
  taken to absurdity; the product is the mascot reminder itself. 1.7 billion impressions on almost no budget (the Q1 2025 shareholder
  letter).
- **Liquid Death (2019).** Water is advertised as death metal: a parody of the genre that breaks the category's main convention.
- **Muzz "POV: A hijabi hit your car" (UGC).** The first 3 s are a conflict without a word about the product; the product arrives
  as a twist: "want to get married, download Muzz". revenuecat.com (blog on UGC ads for apps)
- **Jumpspeak (UGC, podcast).** An "expert" compares the app with a $60-an-hour tutor: price against a pile in the guise of a
  podcast.
- **Picsart (creators, 2025).** Every video is one function that is interesting to watch: the function is the hook.
- **Adobe Express "Jhatphat Fatafat" (2026).** An astrologer tears apart a tasteless invitation and at once makes it better:
  "before/after" in frame, the product shown by its result.
- **Envato "Creative Flow: Activated" (2026).** A designer is calm amid absurd chaos; the whole video is built from
  Envato's own assets: the product is the proof. The offer: one subscription instead of a set.

What from this applies to a reward-based ad on a catalog page:
- The first 3-5 s: a hero and a situation understood without sound; the pitch no later than the 5th second.
- One phrase that the viewer is waiting for a download removes the irritation: from the hero, not as a caption.
- The product as a result or a spectacle: a frame built from the product's own icons and illustrations, the function as a trick.
- One growing thought, not three messages.

## Breakdown: a seasonal-sale reward-based ad on a catalog page

The offer: an annual plan for $99 until the end of the month (afterward $139), a wheel on the sale page picks a bonus for the year. The viewer:
a free user, presses Download and watches the video for the sake of an icon.

Rejected:
- 10 metaphor ideas (a fortune cookie, a weather forecast, a golf replay, a receipt, a lottery ticket, a magnifier over the wheel's
  sectors): "they do not hook at all, detached from reality". An object illustrates the mechanic, there is no person and no situation;
  "$180 vs $99" was taken from the brief although it tells a free user nothing: "did you get into the offers at all?"
- 10 ideas "from the viewer" (you watch for one icon, put the screen together without pauses, under $9 a month, $99 until the end of the month then $139,
  a screenshot to a colleague, no code...): "stale". They all share one skeleton: a screen recording, a caption with the price, a wheel, a logo. It is a list of
  facts, not a story.
- 8 variants about a programmer ("bought icons for a year for $99", "no designer on the team", "unboxing the purchases"):
  "somewhere not there", "stale". The hero simply pronounces the offer. And a mistake about the product: "it is not only icons that go
  for the price, there is a bundle, study the pricing".

Liked:
- A lottery host spins the drum, "You win!" three times in a row, "a lottery you cannot lose", in the end a subscription and bonuses
  ("the first ideas are spun up"). A parody of a format + the rule of three + the offer as the resolution.
- UGC as a whole: "You already pay 15 dollars, what if I offer you to save?": a direct address to the segment.
- The direction "a guy bought a pile for $99", on condition that the pile is true (a bundle).

## Lessons from owner edits (generalized)
- Idea and hook: the hook names a benefit, not news or a lead-in. The angle is a boost to an existing process, not whining and not "save me",
  and not from the position of an unhappy hero. An idea must match the viewer's real behavior and the business (nobody orders "through friends").
  The hero comes from the real audience, not from the demo data in screenshots. Do not promise what the product does not do. No jokes on gender stereotypes: comedy comes
  from the situation, not from the person. An example the owner showed is not a requirement.
- References: take the technique (smoothness, pace, transitions), not the plot; do not copy the moves of old videos, they age.
- Structure and pace: get to the action fast; the product opens at once, the launch icon and the click only at the end; the story goes top to bottom
  with no going back; do not pad the length; no empty stretches; the pace is brisk; every element on screen does something; the finale is the result
  in big with no extra clicks; UGC only as an insert at the start and the end, the body is a demonstration of functions.
- Voice-over and text: headings are not banal, they sound like a living person; no period in a one-sentence heading or subtitle; the text is approved by the owner
  before generation and editing; variants are sent as a list in chat; the voice-over says the action and the context; do not name a concrete search query in a line;
  an exact meaning of the wording; the UGC hero's tone is calm, sharing an insight, not delight; the hero's opener is the "before" pain; the end card says what the product does,
  a slogan that cannot be read in 1.8 s drops out; subtitles match the speech; numbers and names only from the site or analytics.
- On screen: only existing states of the product, no inventions of your own; pieces of interface, not whole windows; show the most popular, not the favorite;
  modern metaphors and photos; the picture is in sync with the word; the hero is in frame only in the hook; do not put complex things into video generation;
  the hero's frame is amateur shooting, not a stock studio; illustration styles are fresh.
- Process: ideas in two paragraphs with no prompts or storyboard until a choice; do not run ahead; scripts in chat as separate messages, in full; edits made
  in chat go into the file; rewrite a prompt whole, not by patches, and without negations in it; do exactly what was asked; agree the key frame before generating video;
  retell the understanding before starting; paid generations only with consent; send nothing outside without an explicit "yes".
